## 2. Explicitly verify encryption of personal details

Encryption must be audited separately from ordinary schema changes. Do not assume that adding soft-delete fields or changing foreign keys provides encryption.

### `mr_directory`

### `doctor_directory`
Review names, phone numbers, email addresses, addresses, and other personal information.

### `patient_directory`
Review names, phone numbers, email addresses, dates of birth, addresses, and other personal information.

For MR, Doctor, and Patient records:
- Identify each sensitive field and whether it is encrypted, plaintext, or unknown.
- Check the prior implementation and task history to determine whether encryption was actually implemented or only identified as needed.
- Do not falsely label additional recommendations as completed requirements.
- Report which fields need encryption and whether new columns, blind indexes, or migration steps are required.

### Encryption implementation and key management
- Verify authenticated encryption, nonce/IV handling, and correct decryption behavior.
- Check encryption key configuration and safe key-rotation considerations.
- Ensure keys are not hardcoded or stored in the database alongside ciphertext.
- Verify necessary searches and uniqueness checks use suitable blind indexes or safe lookup mechanisms.
- Check API responses, logs, error messages, exports, and frontend/backend transformations for unintended exposure of sensitive data.
- Never expose actual encryption keys or plaintext personal data in logs or this report.
- Do not remove plaintext columns until migration, lookups, and all dependent workflows have been verified.
- Confirm existing records can be migrated without data loss and that the application can decrypt the resulting values correctly.

Report encryption status field by field, not merely table by table.

## 3. Verify migrations and database integrity

- Inspect all relevant Alembic revisions and their dependency chain.

- Check foreign keys, indexes, uniqueness constraints, nullability, column types, and deletion behavior.
